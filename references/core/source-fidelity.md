# Core Contract: Source Fidelity

- Treat user-designated source material and user-specified order as the authority for facts, structure, terminology, certainty, and scope.
- Preserve original names, terminology, classifications, qualifications, units, dates, and distinctions whenever feasible. Paraphrase only when clarity improves without changing meaning or evidence strength.
- Preserve sentence-level causal, procedural, conditional, and role relationships. Do not reduce an explanation to disconnected nouns, abbreviations, slogans, or component chains that obscure how the source says the parts interact.
- Give each major work or method a neutral, source-faithful one-sentence summary as an entry point. Preserve a supplied summary where feasible; if synthesis is necessary, make it no stronger than the cited evidence and do not use it as a substitute for the full explanation.
- Keep evidence, author interpretation, user interpretation, uncertainty, limitations, and unsupported possibilities visibly distinct. Titles and takeaways must not exaggerate certainty.
- Do not invent claims, conclusions, experiments, completed work, plans, examples, data, causal links, visual evidence, or filler to satisfy a layout. Remove or change unsupported template slots instead.
- Prefer complete source coverage and readable slide splitting over omission, over-compression, or an artificially low slide count.
- Distinguish reusable-template placeholders from final report language. In module PPTX assets, use semantic field labels such as `分组标题`, `要点标题`, and `要点说明` instead of concrete research text; in an actual report, replace those labels with complete source-faithful wording rather than leaving the result as keyword fragments.
