# Reusable Revision Invariants

Use this checklist to translate presentation feedback into task-independent rules. Apply only the items relevant to the current source and user request; do not copy task-specific research claims into another deck.

## 1. Narrative and chapter structure

- Preserve the cover contract: `学术汇报` is the topmost label; the bottom-right corner shows the reporting year and month; and the right side contains one topic-specific English keyword or short key phrase per line in ALL CAPS.
- Do not replace the right-side keywords with a generic report-type label. Keywords must come from the actual topic or supplied sources and remain visually separated line by line.
- When the user explicitly defines multiple chapters or parts, the opening sequence is `封面 → 全局总括页 → 第一章章内目录`. The whole-deck overview names every chapter in order and briefly states what each chapter covers.
- Keep the whole-deck overview and chapter agendas as separate orientation levels. The overview explains the full report; each chapter agenda explains that chapter's internal contents.
- Do not group all chapter agendas immediately after the whole-deck overview. Each agenda belongs at the start of its own chapter and is followed by that chapter's content before the next chapter agenda appears.
- Preserve the user-specified chapter and subsection order. Do not mix chapters or move a study into a neighboring category to make a layout convenient.
- Give every substantial chapter its own opening agenda, then retain a changing chapter mini-directory on later pages.
- Audit that no requested chapter agenda, subsection, category, study, figure, training workflow, or inference workflow disappeared during revision.
- Use semantic navigation labels. Replace empty codes such as `方式一`, `A/B`, or unexplained abbreviations with the actual function or topic.
- If meaningful labels or independently requested units do not fit, split the directory or add pages. Do not merge units or shrink labels until they become opaque.
- When a content page is visually tight at readable size, split it into continuation pages. Do not treat the original page count as fixed unless the user explicitly requires it.
- For formal paper introductions, default to one paper per page when each work has its own summary, problem, method, role, workflow, or meaning. Keep multiple-paper aggregation only for agendas, taxonomies, surveys, or explicit comparison pages.

## 2. Scope and source fidelity

- Preserve source- or user-written names, keywords, one-sentence summaries, classifications, causal explanations, and workflow order whenever feasible.
- Keep enough of the source's explanatory wording to reconstruct what the work did. Do not reduce causal or procedural sentences to disconnected nouns, abbreviations, plus signs, or slogans.
- When extra space becomes available after a split or redesign, revisit any earlier compression and restore the source document's readable sentence structure, causal transitions, qualifications, and method names.
- Do not add a limitation, key judgment, comparison, evidence label, or evaluative conclusion merely because a template contains a slot for it. Include it only when the user requested it or it is necessary to preserve the source's argument.
- Treat `一句话概括` as the orientation layer, not the entire explanation. Follow it with the source-supported problem, implementation, workflow, meaning, or boundary appropriate to the requested depth.
- Never make the deck intentionally skeletal to save time, tokens, compute, or slide count. Manage complexity by producing and reviewing one chapter at a time.

## 3. Visual hierarchy and grouping

- Visual grouping must match the source's logical grouping. If the source has two parent groups, use two parent regions rather than four unrelated cards; if points are siblings, give them equal visual weight.
- Do not let a convenient template invent primary and secondary importance among parallel points. Adapt the frame or create a style-consistent variant.
- Do not stack unrelated text blocks into leftover space. Every panel, sidebar, caption, and bottom strip must have a named explanatory role tied to the slide's main question.
- A separated right-side explanation is useful for a source-faithful one-sentence orientation, distinction, or beginner-friendly interpretation, but it must explain the main visual rather than introduce another topic.
- If content does not fit at readable size, split it across pages. Do not overlap text, reduce figures to thumbnails, or create dense card mosaics.
- A split is incomplete if each new page still carries the old compressed wording. Use the additional space to restore readable source-faithful explanations.

## 4. Sibling-page consistency

- Use one layout family and stable field order for sibling directions, categories, or papers. The research name and one-sentence summary should occupy the same visual level across the set.
- When a later chapter or revision establishes a clearer reusable layout for a page type, perform a backward-consistency audit and update earlier sibling pages of the same type. Do not leave early paper introductions in a dense legacy format merely because they were created before the improved pattern.
- Apply the same workflow layout to sibling training or inference pages unless the source structure genuinely differs.
- Keep page titles, mini-directory position, source footer, page number, image caption, and explanatory field labels consistent across the chapter.
- Consistency does not require identical content volume. Adjust field height or split pages while preserving the shared hierarchy.
- After splitting a multi-paper page, preserve one shared paper-page family across the new pages: prominent research name, source-faithful one-sentence summary, then the same ordered explanatory fields. Do not replace the former congestion with several unrelated layouts.

## 5. Figures and paper explanation

- Show a paper's source figure large enough that its important labels and structure can be understood during projection.
- When the figure and full explanation cannot coexist legibly, enlarge or crop the relevant panel, or create a separate figure/detail page.
- Coordinate image and text: the explanatory fields must interpret the displayed figure or the paper's central method, not repeat unrelated facts beside it.
- A paper sequence should normally proceed as overview → key mechanism or interface when needed → training → inference → comparison or evidence boundary when requested.

## 6. Process, components, and arrows

- A component inventory does not explain time or causality. When order matters, show stages, inputs, updated or frozen modules, outputs, and runtime removals.
- Use a component view only for genuinely parallel architecture or when architecture comparison is itself the explanatory goal.
- Every `A → B` must represent a source-supported transition, transformation, dependency, or information flow.
- Validate an arrow together with its explanation: read `A`, the arrow label, `B`, and the adjacent prose as one sentence. If they do not describe the same relationship, remove the arrow and use numbered stages, paired fields, a comparison, or prose.
- Do not add arrows merely to decorate a row of terms. For temporal order, numbered steps are usually clearer; for non-directional differences, use a table or paired structure.

## 7. Final revision audit

- Inspect the cover for the exact top label `学术汇报`, a bottom-right year-month value, and a right-side vertical list of topic-specific ALL-CAPS English keywords.
- If the report has multiple explicit chapters or parts, confirm that a whole-deck overview appears immediately after the cover and that the first chapter still retains its own chapter agenda.
- Confirm that later chapter agendas are distributed at their actual chapter boundaries instead of being stacked together in the front matter.
- Compare the final slide inventory against the requested order and source ledger.
- Compare every sibling-page set side by side for layout and field-order consistency.
- Compare early and late instances of each recurring page type; confirm that an improved later pattern was propagated backward where applicable.
- Inspect each source figure at presentation size rather than only in a montage.
- Audit every arrow and connector for semantic agreement with nearby prose.
- Search visible text for unreplaced placeholders, internal codes, `方式一/二`, drafting labels, and accidental component-chain residue.
- Confirm every slide has a source line and a complete notes script, and that no notes text leaked onto the visible canvas.
- Run overflow, structural, and template-fidelity checks after the final visual revision.
